## Abstract

For over eight decades, the electronic design paradigm has been bounded by the thermal requirements of high-power computing, mandating rigid, flame-retardant fiberglass-epoxy laminates (FR4), heavy copper heat-spreading planes, and active cooling envelopes. In this work, we demonstrate that when discrete stack-machine architectures (such as the Regulus K8 core) achieve sub-milliwatt dissipation ($42\,\mu\text{W}$ at $1.2\,\text{MHz}$), the fundamental thermal flux drops to $q'' \approx 9.09\,\text{W/m}^2$, resulting in a surface temperature rise of $\Delta T < 0.001^{\circ}\text{C}$. This thermodynamic inversion completely eliminates the necessity for conventional circuit boards.

We formalize and evaluate five unconventional physical computing substrates:
1. **Stratospheric Pico-Balloons:** A $1.5\text{-gram}$ sovereign computing payload on $12\,\mu\text{m}$ aluminized Mylar with a $500\text{-km}$ line-of-sight LoRa mesh horizon.
2. **Plant Cellulose & Rag Paper:** Living Forth codices with tactile Andean Yupana calculating sensels fabricated via laser-induced graphene (LIG) and screen-printed silver inks.
3. **Woven E-Textiles:** Silver-plated nylon yarns and sinusoidal crimp geometry building strain-decoupled wearable computing garments.
4. **Myco-Electronics:** Fungal mycelium biopolymer skins (*Ganoderma lucidum*) with transient zinc metallization for 60-day compostable ecological sensors.
5. **Architectural Glass & Ceramics:** Transparent conductive oxides (ITO) and surface acoustic wave (SAW) transducers for invisible ambient infrastructure.

All substrates interface seamlessly through the scale-invariant 64-byte CordLink protocol (CLP-64), establishing an unconstrained, sovereign computing ecology embedded directly into the physical environment.
